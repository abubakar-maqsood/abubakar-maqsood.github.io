Research concept · Not yet a supervised study

Evidence-aware uncertainty in language models.

A proposed direction for graduate study in trustworthy AI, language-model evaluation, and evidence-grounded decision making.

Download research concept note ↓Projects ↗
Question

When should an AI system answer, verify, or abstain?

Language models sometimes produce fluent answers despite missing, weak, or contradictory supporting evidence. The central question is whether a lightweight evidence-checking process can improve the system's decisions without simply withholding useful answers.

Proposed experimental design

  • Data: construct controlled questions supplied with relevant, incomplete, or conflicting evidence passages.
  • Baselines: compare direct answer generation, prompt-only abstention instructions, and an explicit evidence-checking step.
  • Outcomes: evaluate factual support, appropriate abstention, unnecessary abstention, calibration if measured, response time, and error categories.
  • Analysis: examine failure cases and trade-offs, ideally on held-out examples with transparent annotations.

What would constitute progress?

A reproducible evaluation showing which failures an evidence-aware approach addresses, where it still fails, and whether its benefits justify the added complexity. This is a research plan, not a claim that the method already works.
